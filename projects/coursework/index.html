Fun Coursework

I've taken some pretty fun and cool classes at UCLA, specifically when I was pursuing my M.S. degree. Here is just a selection of some of the work and projects that I really enjoyed doing.

MAE 279: Dynamics and Control of Biological Oscillations

Spring 2026, Instructor: teD Iwasaki

This was a really cool course! The focus on the class was modelling neuronal circuits through the lens of nonlinear systems, and then the control of them using coupled oscillators. Other topics in the course included network design, optimal gaits, and distributed control.

Assignments + Final Project

ECE M242A: Nonlinear Dynamic Systems

Winter 2026, Instructor: Paulo Tabuada

Very hard class. Goes through a number of techniques for the solutions and controls of nonlinear dynamic systems through a very thorough mathematical perspective. Some of the topics developed in the course included Input-to-State Stability, Control Lyapunov Functions, and Control Barrier Functions.

Assignments

MAE C274: Digital Control and Learning of Physical Systems

Fall 2025, Instructor: Tsu-Chin Tsao

This course felt well-integrated with actual application of control theory to real world systems. The course is taught from the perspective of classical controls, where we first learn how to transform our description of a system into the discrete-time realm and related fundamental concepts (sampling and zero-order holds). From there, we cover different control schemes (Direct\Indirect Digital Control, Observer-based Control) and other relevant techniques both in theory and in practice with a physical system. Some of the topics covered were Disturbance Rejection, Recursive Least Squares, and Adaptive Filtering.

Relevant Assignments

MAE 270C: Optimal Control

Spring 2026, Instructor: Shahriar Talebi

This course navigated through optimal control from the perspective of Calculus of Variations. The course had a focus on applying variational methods and Pontryagin's maximum principle to nonliear dynamic systems to derive optimal solutions. Derivation of the LQR and "bang-bang" controllers and proof of their optimality also performed.

Final Project

MAE 271B: Stochastic Estimation

Winter 2026, Instructor: Shahriar Talebi

This course is rooted in Brownian motion/Wiener process and utilizes Ito calculus to derive the Kalman-Bucy filter in continuous time dynamic systems. The derivation of the filter is applied to simulated GPS recievers to estimate the state and bias of a drone.
